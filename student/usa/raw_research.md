## 1.2  Which Visa Is Right for Pakistani Students?
Almost all Pakistani university, college, and language students use the F-1 visa. M-1 is for vocational schools only. J-1 is primarily for US government-sponsored, Fulbright, or official exchange programs. This brief focuses mainly on F-1 as it applies to the widest group of students.

SECTION 3  —  Form I-20 Requirement

Form I-20 is the Certificate of Eligibility for Nonimmigrant (F-1) Student Status. It is issued by the SEVP-certified school's Designated School Official (DSO) once the school enters the student's information in SEVIS. The I-20 is the primary document proving acceptance and eligibility for an F-1 visa.


## 3.1  F-2 Dependant I-20
If a spouse or children will live in the US with the F-1 student, each dependant must: (1) receive their own individual Form I-20 from the SEVP-certified school; (2) enrol in SEVIS; and (3) apply for an F-2 visa. F-2 dependants do NOT pay the SEVIS fee.

The consular officer must be convinced the applicant has sufficient financial resources to fund their entire study program without unauthorized work. This is assessed during the visa interview. Financial questions are among the most common sources of 214(b) refusals for Pakistani F-1 applicants.

SECTION 18  —  Visa Duration and Entry Rules

## 18.1  Duration of Status vs Visa Validity
A critical distinction: the F-1 visa stamp in the passport is an ENTRY DOCUMENT — it only allows the student to travel to and request entry into the US. Once inside the US, the student's legal status is 'Duration of Status' (D/S) — meaning they can stay as long as they are actively pursuing their program. The visa can expire while the student is in the US without any problem.

SECTION 19  —  Common Refusal Risks — Especially Section 214(b)

🚫  Section 214(b) of the Immigration and Nationality Act presumes ALL non-immigrant visa applicants intend to immigrate permanently. The applicant MUST overcome this presumption. This is the #1 reason for F-1 visa refusals for Pakistani applicants.
